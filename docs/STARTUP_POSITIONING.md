# Education-first startup hypothesis

SkinSnap's current product is a student/research demonstration, not a clinical service. The following statements are product hypotheses to test, not evidence of demand, revenue or clinical value.

## Problem and initial users

Students and the public encounter skin images without enough context to distinguish image classification from diagnosis. Research demos often obscure supported image types, dataset licenses, class imbalance and uncertainty. The initial target users are instructors, student machine-learning teams and health-education workshops who need an understandable, reproducible way to explore a narrow medical-image task.

## Proposed differentiation

Combine local image processing, a visible supported-domain choice, pre-inference quality feedback, transparent artifacts/evaluation, honest rejection states and source-linked skin education. Existing tools may meet some of these needs; competitor shortcomings are an interview question, not an established fact. Do not claim to be first or better at diagnosis.

## Go-to-market and finance hypothesis

Start with a few consenting university instructors and student societies evaluating the educational workflow. Offer a free public educational library and consider a paid institution teaching/research package with reproducibility materials, instructor guides and support, subject to commercial licensing clearance. Possible distribution channels are course demonstrations, research clubs and nonclinical outreach. Prices, conversion, customer acquisition costs and market size remain unmeasured. There are no claimed paying customers or partnerships.

Validate comprehension and practical use with interviews and supervised usability sessions. Track whether users understand image-domain limitations, reject false reassurance and can reproduce a documented experiment. Record support/hosting cost before proposing sustainable pricing. Keep product commerce and educational/inference results separate.

## Privacy

The default path processes the selected image in the browser and offers a clear action to remove the local result/image. The optional server mode must disclose the processing host and its operational retention policies. Do not collect names, contacts or images for model improvement by default. Hosting providers may process request metadata; the repository cannot promise their logging configuration.

## Before clinical use

Obtain appropriate rights for datasets/model distribution/commercial use; commission independent external and prospective evaluation, relevant subgroup assessment and clinical review; test robustness and calibration for the intended population and devices; assess real harms and fallback workflows; complete privacy, security and regulatory work with qualified experts. The research benchmark is not evidence of clinical utility. Regulatory status is not asserted here.
