# SkinSnap SCIN photograph research model

Version: `skinsnap-scin-mobilenet-v1`. This is a separate model from the seven-class dermoscopic lesion model. It is an exploratory appearance classifier, not a diagnosis or a validated way to identify the best treatment or skincare product. **Inference is unavailable:** the corrected run fails the declared performance minimums and reuses previously examined test cases. Check `public/model-clinical/manifest.json` for the actual availability gate and `models/clinical/evaluation.json` for measured results.

## Source, permission, and label quality

[SCIN](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin) contains voluntarily donated photographs from adult Google Search users in the US. The released metadata contains 5,033 cases, with up to three images each. Labels are retrospective dermatologist differentials based on contributed images and information, rather than clinically confirmed diagnoses. The study authors explicitly distinguish these labels from diagnosis; see [Google's dataset description](https://research.google/blog/scin-a-new-resource-for-representative-dermatology-images/) and the [published study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11579799/).

Anonymous source URLs are `https://storage.googleapis.com/dx-scin-public-data/dataset/scin_cases.csv`, `scin_labels.csv` at the same prefix, and image paths provided by the metadata. The actual released label column is `dermatologist_skin_condition_on_label_name`; `weighted_skin_condition_label` contains a Python-literal dictionary of normalized differential rank weights. The weights express annotation differential structure, not disease probabilities.

SCIN has a [custom SCIN Data Use License](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin/blob/main/LICENSE), copied verbatim to `models/clinical/licenses/SCIN_LICENSE.txt`. It grants reproduction, sharing, and adaptation subject to its conditions, including attribution and a strict prohibition on attempting to re-identify contributors. It does not provide endorsement, clinical validation, or an unrestricted grant of privacy/personality rights. The model/demo are attributed to SCIN and Google LLC, with image modifications recorded in the demo manifest. No attempt is made to identify contributors.

The feature extractor uses the official [TorchVision MobileNetV3-Small ImageNet1K-V1 weights](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models/generated/torchvision.models.mobilenet_v3_small.html) from `https://download.pytorch.org/models/mobilenet_v3_small-047dcff4.pth`. TorchVision software is BSD 3-Clause; the full software license is copied to `models/clinical/licenses/TORCHVISION_LICENSE.txt`. [TorchVision cautions that pretrained models may inherit dataset terms](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models.html), so a BSD code license must not be presented as commercial clearance for ImageNet-derived weights. This project uses the model for research/education and does not claim commercial rights review is complete. A paid clinical or retail deployment requires its own rights and regulatory assessment.

## Focused labels

The ordered output classes are:

| ID | User-facing appearance category | Source differential mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `acne` | Acne-like appearance | `Acne` differential weight at least 0.50 and greater than grouped dermatitis weight |
| `dermatitis` | Dermatitis-like appearance | Combined `Eczema`, `Allergic Contact Dermatitis`, `Irritant Contact Dermatitis`, `Acute dermatitis, NOS`, and `CD - Contact dermatitis` weight at least 0.50 and greater than acne weight |
| `other` | Other / unsupported appearance | A source condition weight at least 0.50 and combined acne/dermatitis weight less than 0.20 |

Grouping dermatitis is a task design choice. The model cannot distinguish eczema, allergic reactions, or irritant dermatitis, identify a triggering allergen, or decide whether an infection or other diagnosis is present. `other` is a finite mixture of source labels, not a comprehensive disease class, a normal-skin class, or a validated arbitrary-image detector. It is always rejected for condition-driven product matching.

The predeclared five-name mapping is also incomplete: source synonyms such as `Acute and chronic dermatitis` are not included and can fall into `other`. The deterministic other demo has that source differential. This is a taxonomy failure of the present task construction, so the reported test measures agreement with the stated mapping rather than comprehensive dermatitis recognition. It is documented instead of silently relabeling an already observed test partition. A revised mapping needs dermatologist review and a newly frozen independent evaluation cohort.

All cases marked as involving genitalia/groin or buttocks are excluded from this cohort. One image (`image_1_path`) is used per case, with no replication of a case's images to inflate the sample count. All eligible acne cases are used; deterministic hash ordering caps dermatitis at 400 cases and other at 300 cases. Ambiguous/low-weight differentials are excluded. Full exclusion, download-failure, and split counts are saved in the evaluation and cohort records. This creates an artificial class prevalence and must not be treated as a population risk estimate.

## Architecture and exact preprocessing

Frozen TorchVision MobileNetV3-Small convolutional features output `576×4×4` activations for a `128×128` image. Global spatial averaging produces 576 features, followed by a newly trained three-output linear classifier. No generic language model, filename, random prediction, or dermatology prediction copied from a third-party demo is used.

Images are EXIF-transposed, converted to RGBA, composited onto white, and converted to RGB. The full image is resized to a square using explicitly implemented nearest sampling: source coordinate `floor((destination_coordinate + 0.5) * source_size / 128)`, capped to the last pixel. Aspect ratio can change; no lesion crop or face segmentation is inferred. RGB values are divided by 255 and normalized with mean `[0.485, 0.456, 0.406]` and standard deviation `[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]`, then converted to float32 NCHW `[1, 3, 128, 128]`.

This differs from the pretrained model's original 224-pixel center-crop evaluation transform and is explicitly evaluated only for this downstream research task. Low resolution loses diagnostic detail. Class activation maps use the actual folded linear weights and exported convolutional activations; they show contributions to a score, not proof of medical correctness.

## Reproducibility and leakage controls

Run `python scripts/clinical_model_train.py` from the repository root after installing the pinned training requirements in `models/clinical/requirements-training.txt`. The script downloads and caches public source metadata and selected originals in ignored `data/clinical/`; it never uses user uploads. Seed `20261012` controls selection, split ordering, and head fitting. CPU PyTorch uses four threads and deterministic algorithms.

All released case image paths and anonymous GCS MD5 metadata are used to union cases that share exact images, including links through excluded cases. Selected originals also receive SHA-256, normalized-pixel SHA-256, and 64-bit difference hashes. Selected cases within dHash Hamming distance two are grouped before splitting. Conflicting-label duplicate groups are removed. Connected case/duplicate groups are assigned to train, validation, and test in approximately 65/15/20 proportions, stratified by source class. A frozen cohort record is written before feature extraction and model selection. No participant identifier is released, so this is case-disjoint, not proven patient-disjoint. Near-duplicate checks are conservative and scoped to selected first images; they cannot establish absence of all leakage.

The frozen backbone extracts features once. A train-only standard scaler and multinomial logistic regression with balanced class weights are fitted for each predeclared regularization value `C ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}`. Maximum validation macro-F1 selects the head; balanced accuracy breaks ties. The scaler is algebraically folded into the exported linear weights. The corrected test is not used to select regularization, temperature, demo output, or rejection thresholds; however, it is not independent of previously examined data.

The initial experiment had a body-part parsing error: SCIN releases location flags as `YES`/blank, while the initial parser accepted only `true`. It therefore failed the intended sensitive-location exclusions. This was corrected without changing the predefined class mapping, C grid, seed, or performance thresholds, and the cohort and artifacts were regenerated. The initial script, cohort, checkpoint, and evaluation are preserved under `models/clinical/pre_audit_*`; they are historical records, not the served model. **129 of the corrected 148 test cases appeared in the already examined initial test.** The corrected measurements are exploratory; no untouched-holdout claim is made. The current release keeps `independent_holdout=false` and `available=false` even if a future rerun happened to pass its internal metric minimums. Revised task development needs a newly frozen independent evaluation cohort and version.

The demo selector prefers case metadata without a `head_or_neck` flag, rather than verified first-image anatomy. A manual visual check found the acne demo is a cheek close-up even though its released case metadata lists only `other`; this disagreement is recorded in the demo manifest. Its source differential gives equal weight to acne and rosacea. The dermatitis sample is labeled `Allergic Contact Dermatitis`, while the `other` sample is labeled `Acute and chronic dermatitis` and demonstrates the mapping omission. None is a confirmed diagnosis, and none was chosen for a successful prediction.

## Calibration, uncertainty, and readiness

Temperature is selected on validation-only negative log-likelihood from the fixed grid `0.50–3.00` in increments of `0.05`. Exported logits are divided by that temperature before softmax. Scores are temperature-scaled model scores, not medical probabilities. Calibration on a small, selected cohort does not establish clinical calibration or transfer to arbitrary cameras, lighting, age groups, skin tones, or populations.

The fixed research rules require maximum score at least 0.65 and a top-two score margin at least 0.15. `other` is always unable to assess. These rules do not constitute validated out-of-distribution detection or clinically safe triage.

Before the initial evaluation, the engineering performance minimums were declared as macro-F1 at least 0.60, balanced accuracy at least 0.60, acne recall at least 0.50, and dermatitis recall at least 0.50. They are never weakened to make a demonstration pass. Availability also requires an independent evaluation cohort; this release lacks one after the parser repair. Even passing the performance minimums establishes only a modest internal benchmark baseline, not medical readiness or reliable condition identification. Tiny acne test support requires wide uncertainty intervals and additional data.

## Intended and prohibited uses

Intended: student demonstrations and research evaluation of a narrow consumer-photograph appearance task, with honest unable-to-assess states, metadata attribution, and general cosmetic education. Condition-aware cosmetic ranking remains a transparent rule system combining the model's tentative appearance category with stated preferences; it is not a clinical recommender trained on treatment outcomes.

Prohibited claims: clinical diagnosis; disease probabilities; clinical validation; a guaranteed or absolute best product; prescription selection; allergen detection; severity assessment; an all-clear result; excluding cancer or infection; or replacing professional assessment. Do not apply a new cosmetic product to the photographed rash or lesion based solely on this output. Severe, spreading, painful, infected-looking, bleeding, changing, or persistent problems need appropriate professional evaluation irrespective of model output.

Important failure risks include body-location/background shortcuts (acne photographs may disproportionately show head/neck), volunteer/source selection, label ambiguity, small acne support, resolution and aspect-ratio changes, unknown participant overlap, unrepresented conditions, and demographic/skin-tone gaps. Estimated Monk skin tone is available but subgroup counts are small; measured subgroup accuracy cannot establish fairness. No prospective, external-cohort, clinician-user, product-response, or regulatory validation has been completed.

The corrected frozen-feature baseline measured macro-F1 **0.3862**, balanced accuracy **0.3884**, acne recall **1/9 (0.1111)**, and dermatitis recall **53/79 (0.6709)** on its 148-case exploratory test partition. It failed the engineering minimums; `available=false` is retained. The initial pre-repair run separately measured macro-F1 0.3964 and acne recall 1/10. Credible further development requires an appropriately licensed, substantially larger acne/photo cohort, dermatologist-reviewed label grouping, and an independently frozen external holdout. Backbone-training experiments must use development train/validation data; the observed test must be disclosed as previously examined and must not be advertised as untouched in a subsequent model-selection cycle. Failure is not repaired by lowering a gate or choosing a successful-looking image.
